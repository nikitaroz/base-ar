# The electrical "boxes" and what Base asks about each

> Source explainer pasted by Logan on 26 September 2026. Kept verbatim; the checked, organized version is `docs/context/electrical-equipment-guide.md`.

Here's the path electricity takes into your house. Each thing along it is one of the "boxes," and Base asks something different about each.

```
Utility wires ──► ① Electric meter ──► ② Main disconnect ──► ③ Breaker panel ──► your outlets, lights, AC
 (pole or         (outside wall)       (the big MAIN          (rows of small
  underground)                          switch)                 switches)
```

Items ② and ③ are often the same box: the main switch sits at the top of the breaker panel. Sometimes ② is its own small box next to the meter instead.

## ① Electric meter
- What it is: a round glass-covered gauge on an outside wall, owned by the utility (Austin Energy, Oncor and so on).
- What it does: measures how much electricity you use so the utility can bill you. It doesn't switch anything on or off.
- What Base wants:
  - A zoomed-in photo where the meter number is legible. That's the ID number printed on the face, not the usage reading.
  - Wide photos: the area around the meter from about 10 steps back, the areas to its left and right, and the whole wall corner to corner. Base installs the battery near the meter, so these show where it could go.
  - The meter must be no higher than 6 ft, with 30 in × 36 in of clear space in front of it.

## ② Main disconnect (main breaker)
- What it is: the single big switch, usually labeled MAIN. It's either at the top of your breaker panel or in a small separate box next to or under the meter. On some combined meter-and-breaker boxes it's under a small black or gray lid you lift up.
- What it does: shuts off power to the whole house at once. Its number (100, 125, 150 or 200) is the most current your home can draw.
- What Base wants: a sharp close-up where that amperage number can be read. In Austin it must be 150–200 A, and 200 A if you have solar or want two batteries. This is the number we talked about earlier.

## ③ Breaker panel (breaker box)
- What it is: the metal box with a door and rows of switches. It's usually in the garage or on an outside wall.
- What it does: splits power into separate circuits (kitchen, bedrooms, AC). Each small breaker trips if its circuit is overloaded. The small numbers like 15 or 20 aren't the ones Base wants.
- What Base wants:
  - A photo of the box, plus a zoomed-out photo of the area around it.
  - There must be only one main breaker box, and it can't be in a closet.
  - It must share the same wall as the meter, either side by side or on opposite sides of the same wall.
  - It needs the same 30 in × 36 in of clear space in front.

## Other things near the meter that come up
- Gas meter (if you have one): a gray pipe-and-dial unit, often on the same side of the house. The battery must be at least 3 ft away from it. Base doesn't ask for its own photo of it, but it shows up in the wide shots.
- Transfer switch: you don't have one yet. Base installs it beside the meter so the battery can power the house during an outage. There has to be wall space for it next to the meter, about 13 in wide.
- The battery (Base Core): about 31 in W × 36 in H × 22 in D, sitting on a 3 ft × 3 ft spot. It goes within 20 ft of the meter and within 1 ft of the wall.
