# Score the wall before trusting clearances

The outdoor point cloud looks finished long before it is trustworthy, and Apple will not announce the moment it becomes so. Scene reconstruction gives Base Site Survey an updating triangle mesh, optional labels such as wall and floor, and, only on a LiDAR iPhone, a depth image whose confidence is the qualitative trio **low, medium, or high**. There is no coverage percent, no hole query, and no public instruction that names a world point on that mesh. The team decides “enough” by clipping the mesh to a **gravity-aligned box a few meters wide** on the meter wall and the ground in front of it, counting faces seen more than once from a head-on angle at medium or high confidence, and aiming the user at the largest remaining hole with one short line. That grade can support **foot-scale clearances at about 1–5 cm** when the surface sits well inside the scanner’s published **5 m** range and tracking is normal. It cannot certify the **20 ft** walk to the meter, which is longer than one depth sample and is where published scans lose accuracy to the path the phone took. Phones without LiDAR still place the battery from detected planes and Apple’s tracking coach; they have no mesh to score. A box with its holes closed still leaves footprint clearance and transfer-switch space unmeasured, so scan coverage and a green placement preview are different answers.

## Apple coaches the session, and never names a hole

The guidance Apple already ships answers a different question from “is this patch of wall measured.” `ARCoachingOverlayView`, available since iOS 13, shows standardized move-the-phone illustrations until a goal is met, then hides. The goals are **basic world tracking, one horizontal plane, one vertical plane, any plane, or geo-tracking**. Completion is binary: tracking has started, or at least one surface of the requested kind exists. The overlay has no mesh goal, no progress value, and no reaction to scene reconstruction (`([ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview))`, `([Goal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum))`). It is the right onboarding for the first seconds of placement, on LiDAR and non-LiDAR phones alike, and it goes quiet once a ground plane or a wall plane exists. That silence is not a statement about the meter, the pad, or the working space.

Two later capture pipelines look closer and still do not attach to an `ARMeshAnchor` session. RoomPlan, iOS 16 and LiDAR-only, builds a model of an **interior room**. Its public instruction enum has six cases, all semantic rather than geometric: scanning is normal, **move closer to the wall, move farther from the wall, slow down, turn on a light, or the view has too little texture** (a solid-color wall, or a viewfinder with no edges). The framework view decides, by an unpublished rule, that the scan is complete and then shows a miniature room to approve. `.normal` means “no coaching right now,” not “the room is finished.” Nothing in the enum is a ray, a bounding box, or an unscanned face (`([RoomCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcaptureview))`, `([Instruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction))`, `([lowTexture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/lowtexture))`). Apple’s own write-up of the shipping feature says the live sentences are “Turn up the light,” “Slow down,” and “Move farther away,” each from a tiny on-device classifier reported at about **90%** accuracy, and that a five-minute scan is sized for rooms up to **15 m × 15 m**. The article does not describe choosing a viewpoint to fill a hole (`([Apple, RoomPlan](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/roomplan))`).

Object Capture, iOS 17, is an image session aimed at a photogrammetry model. Feedback covers **too close, too far, too dark, low light, moving too fast, object box outside the frame, object not detected, object not worth flipping, and too many shots**. `userCompletedScanPass` becomes true after a **full circular pass** around an object bounding box fills a capture dial. Apple’s 2024 sample, which adds an area mode for surroundings indoors or outdoors, suppresses the move-closer and move-farther lines in that mode and tells the person to look at the subject; the review line is “Points show what will be included in the final model. Continue capturing to include a larger area or process now.” Area mode does not wait on the capture dial. The sample’s own minimum of ten images and its three recommended orbits are app copy, not framework constants (`([Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum))`, `([userCompletedScanPass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/usercompletedscanpass))`, `([Scanning objects using Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/scanning-objects-using-object-capture))`). Reusing `RoomCaptureView` or `ObjectCaptureView` paints Apple’s UI only inside those sessions. It does not annotate the scene-reconstruction mesh this app already shows, and an electrical meter on a house wall cannot be orbited or flipped.

What a plain world-tracking session can read is quality of the coordinate frame and of the current depth image, not a coverage score. `trackingState` is **normal, unavailable, or limited**, and the limited reasons Apple says to present to the user are **initializing, excessive motion, insufficient features, and relocalizing**. In the limited state, anchor positions are not reliable (`([TrackingState](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum))`). `worldMappingStatus` says whether the visible area is mapped well enough to **relocalize a saved world map** later (`notAvailable`, `limited`, `extending`, `mapped`). Mapped means a good candidate for coming back to this view, not that a tape measure would agree (`([worldMappingStatus](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.property))`). On a LiDAR device, `sceneDepth` and `smoothedSceneDepth` add a depth map in meters from the camera plane plus a confidence map. Apple defines the three levels only as **less confident, moderately confident, and fairly confident**, and says to filter out lower-accuracy depths. Natural light makes the framework less confident on surfaces that are highly reflective or that absorb a lot of light. No centimeter error is attached to any level, and the fused mesh has no confidence channel (`([confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap))`, `([ARConfidenceLevel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel))`). `rawFeaturePoints` are explicitly unstable in count from frame to frame; the supported feature signal is the tracking reason `insufficientFeatures`, not a threshold on the integer (`([rawFeaturePoints](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/rawfeaturepoints))`).

The mesh itself is the remaining raw material. With `sceneReconstruction` set to mesh, or to **meshWithClassification**, ARKit delivers `ARMeshAnchor`s whose vertices, faces, and per-vertex normals update as the model is refined. Classification, when present, is one label per face: **none, wall, floor, ceiling, table, seat, window, or door**. Unclassified faces are `none`, which means ARKit cannot name the face. It does not mean the face is missing, and there is no class for an electrical meter, a gas meter, outdoor ground, or sky (`([sceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/scenereconstruction))`, `([ARMeshClassification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification))`). Anchors keep an identity while geometry is replaced, so the app can watch a surface move between updates, but Apple’s own example of a chair being pulled out is explicit that the mesh is **not a real-time tracker of change**. Plane detection, if also on, flattens the mesh on detected planes. People occlusion removes mesh that overlaps a person (`([ARMeshAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshanchor))`). WWDC20 describes the mesh as depth aggregated across frames, with triangle size varying for detail, and shows that demo in a living room (`([WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/))`). The live instruction enums, fetched 26 September 2026, still have no “this region is incomplete” case.

## Clip the mesh to a few meters of wall and ground

Classic next-best-view planning picks a camera pose by scoring unseen volume or information gain, and that machinery is the wrong prototype here. The formulations that ray-cast through a probabilistic voxel map assume a **bounded object**, often with no ground plane, and they were timed on robot computers: one comparison reports well under ten seconds per view for a half-meter object in a one-meter cube at one-centimeter resolution, with most of the surface appearing in fewer than about ten good views (`([Delmerico et al. 2017](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/AURO17_Delmerico.pdf))`). The only phone timing in these notes is a 2013 research app that planned one view in about **900 ms** on an iPhone 5, using a 13-cell grid around a sparse photo cloud, then spent **2–20 seconds** integrating each new still (`([Langguth and Goesele 2013](https://doi.org/10.2312/conf/eg2013/short/093-096))`). A three-meter working volume at one-centimeter cells is on the order of eighteen million voxels. ARKit does not hand the app the occupancy probabilities those scores need. Learned planners from 2020 through 2026 remain robot systems. They are not a user instruction.

An open yard also breaks the stop rule those planners use. Unknown-voxel and frontier scores keep proposing sky, street, and overhead viewpoints a person on the ground cannot take. Surface Edge Explorer states the remedy directly: **define a scene boundary and discard measurements outside it**, because otherwise “no frontiers left” assumes a finite scene (`([Border, Gammell, and Newman 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08617))`). Room-closure rules fail for the same reason. Waiting for a ceiling, or for walls to form a loop, never finishes on one façade. RoomPlan’s published system is interior walls, doors, windows, floor, ceiling, and indoor furniture (`([Apple, RoomPlan](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/roomplan))`). magicplan’s Auto-Scan, which sits on RoomPlan, will still try to close a room and then drops an orange wall from the 2D plan (`([magicplan Auto-Scan](https://help.magicplan.app/auto-scan-your-floor-plan))`). Twindo will sometimes reject an open-space scan only later, in a CAD pipeline, while also accepting a single exterior wall as a model priced by wall area (`([Twindo exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/))`, `([Twindo rejection](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/))`).

The baseline that matches both the literature and a hackathon is therefore a clip, not a reconstruction. Fix a gravity-aligned box on the order of **3 m wide, 3 m deep, and 2 m tall**, anchored to the wall and the ground in front of it, and ignore mesh outside it. That box is the equipment neighborhood (the pad, the meter, a few feet of working space), not the yard. Each frame, take faces inside the box that project into the image. Count a face as a good observation only while `trackingState` is normal, the view is not edge-on to the face normal, and, when scene depth exists, the confidence sample is medium or high. Faces with a low count, and boundary edges owned by only one triangle, are the hole set. Cluster them. The instruction target is the centroid of the largest cluster, converted into a ground-plane direction and one verb. Stop when that cluster is small relative to the pad, or when the area of faces that have passed the view-count and angle test **stops growing for a few seconds**. Require some qualifying wall faces and some qualifying floor faces before calling the structural surfaces in. Do not require a ceiling, a closed wall loop, or a class for the meter. The meter and the gas meter are covered only to the extent they fall inside the box and produce mesh (`([Border, Gammell, and Newman 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08617))`, `([Pito 1999](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.799908))`, `([Massios and Fisher 1998](https://doi.org/10.5244/c.12.78))`).

A coarser occupancy grid is the same idea in a form that is cheap to update. Cell size is a heuristic. Converting published raw-return densities to a uniform spacing gives about **1.2 cm at 25 cm** range and about **8.2 cm at 2.5 m**; those figures are not measured `ARMeshAnchor` edge lengths, and Apple does not publish vertex spacing (`([Luetzenburg, Kroon, and Bjørk 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-01763-9))`). A **5–10 cm** cell sits in the same range as that far-field spacing, the roughly 5 cm object-detection floor in the same paper, and the 5 cm per-pixel disagreement Apple’s ARKitScenes authors used when discarding bad indoor depth frames against a Faro scan (`([arXiv:2111.08897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08897))`). For each occupied cell, count frames where the cell projects into the image, the camera is within a few meters, tracking is normal, and confidence is medium or high. Compare the depth sample with the mesh point’s camera-plane distance, because `depthMap` is z from the camera plane, not slant range. A residual larger than about **5 cm** is a reasonable “present but disagreeing” flag, borrowed from that indoor filter, not from an outdoor calibration. Pair occupancy with a horizontal or vertical normal. Use `wall` as supporting evidence for the house wall. Treat `floor` as optional for outdoor concrete or soil, because no retrieved source measures whether ARKit labels those surfaces floor, and geometric evidence still stands when the class is `none`.

Two failure modes have to stay visible in that score. A glass meter cover, glossy paint, wet concrete, or very dark siding is exactly the reflective or absorptive case Apple ties to low confidence, and specular or transparent surfaces are what ARKitScenes calls depth aggressors that are hard to reject automatically (`([confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap))`, `([arXiv:2111.08897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08897))`). Faces the sensor will never instantiate look like holes forever. The confidence map is the only public hint that distinguishes “not scanned yet” from “this surface will not mesh,” and it only covers the current view. A person walking through the shot either lags in the mesh or, with people occlusion on, punches a hole. Freeze a clearance only after the relevant cells stay put across updates while tracking remains normal. The minimum view count, the grazing-angle cutoff, the hole-size limit, and the seconds of diminishing returns are **not published for this scene**. They are tuned on a device. No retrieved paper times this guider on an iPhone.

## One imperative and a tint on the empty patch

Shipping products show the unscanned part and leave the finish button in the user’s hand. Polycam’s LiDAR Space mode draws a **blue grid** on surfaces not yet captured and replaces it with white mesh; help says to tap Done when no blue grid remains, and it does not say the button is locked until then. The as-built write-up is blunt about finishing early: the app will guess, and dimensions will be less accurate. The same help says a second pass over an area already captured introduces artifacts (`([Polycam Space mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices))`, `([Polycam as-builts](https://poly.cam/blog/how-to-3d-scan-as-builts-with-polycam))`). magicplan turns a recognized wall **green with a checkmark** and leaves a poorly scanned wall **orange**, then offers Confirm or Discard & Rescan (`([magicplan Auto-Scan](https://help.magicplan.app/auto-scan-your-floor-plan))`). Scaniverse starts blurry and sharpens where useful angles exist, and tells the person to keep going on what is still blurry, with a published sweet spot of **1–3 minutes** before quality can decline (`([Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support))`). SiteScape’s live instruction is a spray-can metaphor at **3–12 ft (1–4 m)**, with a progress bar that ends a segment because position drifts, not because a hole map filled (`([SiteScape best practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices))`). Twindo’s room path is fence-painting: up and down the wall, step sideways, one direction, then Done (`([Twindo room scan](https://www.twindo.com/support/tutorial-how-to-scan-a-room-with-canvas/))`). None of the help articles retrieved publish a mesh-density percentage that unlocks Done.

The sentence this app should show is one line, replaced rather than stacked. Copy the words Apple and these products already trained people on, and invent a direction only for the hole the box actually found.

| Condition the app can see | Line to show | Where the wording comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracking limited, excessive motion | Slow down. | RoomPlan `.slowDown`; Object Capture sample “Move slower” |
| Tracking limited, insufficient features, or the camera is on blank siding or sky | Point at the meter or a corner. | Tracking reason plus RoomPlan `.lowTexture`; SiteScape’s blank-wall warning |
| Largest hole sits to one side along the wall | Step left along the wall. / Step right along the wall. | App text. The direction is the hole centroid on the ground plane |
| Hole is far, or the only views are edge-on | Move closer. | Object Capture sample “Move Closer”; RoomPlan “move closer to the wall” |
| Phone is so close that the frame is only blank wall | Step back. | RoomPlan “move farther from the wall”; Twindo’s too-close tracking loss |
| Camera aimed into the sun | Turn so the sun is behind you. | Twindo and Polycam outdoor guidance; Object Capture’s “keep the sun out of your images” is for photos |
| Qualifying wall and ground inside the box have stopped growing | This wall and the ground in front of it are covered. | App text. Area-mode review already tells the user the points are what will be kept |

Show the failing faces inside the box with a single tint, the way Polycam’s grid marks what is still empty. Leave the path back to the hub available. A false “complete” is the worse failure, because a guessed surface still looks like a model. Haptics can mean frames are being kept, which is what Polycam’s buzz means. A haptic or a percent that means “accurate to the inch” overclaims every source that was willing to put a number on error. Do not ask for a second scrub of a patch already marked seen. Do not ask the user to walk around the meter when a fence or shrubs block the far side. Do not copy magicplan’s “stay in the same spot,” which is an indoor room trick, or Object Capture’s orbit, flip, and turntable. A green check on the patch means the patch was observed. It does not mean the placement rules passed, and it should not appear because a hole was filled in by the app.

The same banner carries the outdoor capture advice these vendors repeat, as separate triggers from the hole arrow. Stay about **1–4 m** off the wall for coverage, and step in when the number that matters is a close clearance. Move the body and keep the phone’s rotation small. Keep a feature in frame: the meter, a corner, a window edge. Polycam names too much sunlight or moving too fast as a way to lose tracking, and tells people to avoid strong directional sun. SiteScape says to avoid outdoors and, above about **75°F**, to expect a hot phone to throttle. Twindo’s September 2026 page is milder: bright sun matters less on current LiDAR iPhones than on older sensors, and it is still necessary to avoid shooting into the sun (`([Polycam Space mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices))`, `([SiteScape best practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices))`, `([Twindo exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/))`). Apple’s own pages do not say scene reconstruction turns off in sunlight. They do say natural light lowers depth confidence on reflective and absorptive surfaces (`([confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap))`). Treat aiming into the sun as a tracking risk the banner can name. A one-to-two-minute pass over this patch sits inside Scaniverse’s 1–3 minute window and well under Twindo’s advice to keep a whole-floor scan under about ten minutes.

The only research UI that actually pointed a person at the next pose is Langguth and Goesele’s: an arrow in the image, a distance, and a ghost photo to align with, firing when the view was within **10°** of the plan. Navigation took **5–30 seconds**, longer when the ghost was vague, and on an outdoor statue the planner proposed viewpoints above the ground that had to be rejected (`([Langguth and Goesele 2013](https://doi.org/10.2312/conf/eg2013/short/093-096))`). Their ghost depends on feature patches and fails on a textureless wall, which is the common exterior. The arrow-plus-one-verb is the piece worth keeping. The ghost image is not.

## A foot can be inch-class; twenty feet stays coarser

Apple publishes no pass/fail for “dense enough,” and the measurement literature supports a graded reading rather than a single cloud-wide accuracy. The scanner’s only Apple-stated range is the 18 March 2020 iPad Pro announcement: objects **up to 5 meters** away, indoors and outdoors (`([Apple Newsroom, 18 March 2020](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2020/03/apple-unveils-new-ipad-pro-with-lidar-scanner-and-trackpad-support-in-ipados/))`). The iPhone 12 Pro newsroom post and the WWDC20 scene-geometry transcript do not restate that figure, and no 2024–2026 Apple spec page retrieved here updates it. Vendors still treat 5 m as the sensor limit in 2026: Polycam’s depth-range control maxes at 5 m “set by the range of the LiDAR sensor,” Twindo’s exterior page says **5 m (about 16 ft 5 in)** with accuracy declining toward that end, and Scaniverse points people at splats when they need to go beyond 5 m (`([Polycam Space mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices))`, `([Twindo exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/))`, `([Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support))`). `supportsSceneReconstruction` is false without a LiDAR scanner, so the mesh, the depth image, and the confidence map are absent together (`([supportsSceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/supportsscenereconstruction(_:)))`).

Close-range results are the ones that match a person standing at a wall. Luetzenburg, Kroon, and Bjørk, scanning with an iPhone 12 Pro and an iPad Pro through apps that **sample points from the mesh rather than exporting raw returns**, report about **±1 cm** absolute accuracy for objects larger than about 10 cm, precision worsening below that, and a detection limit around **5 cm**. On a smaller outdoor rock patch, about 10 × 15 × 10 m, the mean distance between repeat scans was **0.02 m**, and **92%** of points were within 5 cm of a reference LiDAR model. The same sensor family against photogrammetry on a 130 m cliff is a different problem: M3C2 root-mean-square **0.69 m**, 80% of points within 15 cm and 92% within 30 cm, while the abstract’s ±10 cm is a looser summary of that same product. Density in their series falls from **7,225 points per square meter at 25 cm** to **150 at 2.5 m**, and differences grew with range and on rough or sloped surfaces (`([Luetzenburg, Kroon, and Bjørk 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-01763-9))`). Costantino and colleagues’ abstract puts smartphone depth, including an iPhone 12 Pro, at about **1–3 cm** when scanning problems were absent, and names drift and loss of planarity as the failure modes; the paper’s tables were not in the text retrieved here (`([Costantino et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/asi5040063))`). Treccani, Adami, and Fregonese, comparing iOS LiDAR apps with a terrestrial laser scanner in a corridor and a narrow street, report errors on the order of **1 cm in some areas and 10 cm in others**, with apps that closed the trajectory loop doing better (`([Treccani, Adami, and Fregonese 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-xlviii-2-w8-2024-431-2024))`). Store copy that says “inch” or “millimeter” is marketing. Twindo’s own accuracy page claims most processed CAD measurements within **1–2%** of a tape in their case studies, refuses any universal millimeter bound, and tells users to tape the few dimensions that must be tighter (`([Twindo accuracy](https://www.twindo.com/support/what-kind-of-accuracy-can-i-expect-from-canvas/))`).

Read against this app’s checks, the defensible grades are these. **Missing:** no triangles in the region that matters, a hole on the order of the 3 ft footprint, the surface never brought inside a few meters, or tracking not normal while the only samples were taken. A gap near 5 cm can be the sensor’s floor rather than a real opening. **Uncertain:** triangles exist from a single view, confidence on the rays that hit the surface is mostly low, successive anchor updates still move the surface by more than the tolerance of the check, or depth and mesh disagree by more than about 5 cm. Classification `none` on a surface the survey would like to call wall or ground makes the label useless and leaves the geometry usable. **Locally usable for a foot or a few feet:** the wall, the ground under the pad, and nearby clearance objects sit well inside 5 m, ideally in the close range where the small-object work saw about 1 cm and the small-patch work saw a few centimeters; more than one normal-tracking view; confidence at least medium on the samples used. A 1–5 cm error still answers “about 1 ft from the wall” or “about 3 ft from the gas meter.” It does not answer a millimeter call.

The **20 ft** meter distance is about **6.1 m**. That is past a single 5 m depth sample, so it is a walked baseline. Smooth motion can keep `trackingState` normal the whole way while the integrated path drifts. Treccani’s 10 cm-class regions are the right warning for a medium outdoor walk, and the cliff-scale RMS is the warning for treating a long, rough trajectory as local LiDAR noise. Overlapping views of the same surfaces reduce that drift; ARKit does not report a drift estimate, and `worldMappingStatus` is not a substitute. The honest label for that distance, unless the path was reobserved, is **about a tenth of a meter**. Calling the preview inch-accurate across the whole cloud overclaims the literature. No retrieved study tapes an ARKit scene-reconstruction mesh of a house wall and a meter at 1–6 m outdoors. The numbers above are cliffs, streets, rooms, boxes, and indoor laser comparisons.

On a phone where `supportsSceneReconstruction` is false, do not invent a mesh grade and do not mark the cloud thin. Report mesh completeness as **unavailable**. Placement continues from plane anchors, raycasts against those planes, and `trackingState`. The coaching overlay’s tracking and plane goals still apply. `insufficientFeatures` on a blank wall and `excessiveMotion` when the phone is swung too fast remain the user-visible quality signals. A graded “the mesh is dense enough” answer does not exist on that device, and a non-LiDAR survey should say so in the review export.

## Conclusion

Scan completeness is a property of a box the team draws, computed from faces, view angles, and depth confidence Apple already delivers, and it should be stored beside each clearance as missing, uncertain, locally usable, or unavailable. The placement color already means something else: green only when every required check has measured evidence and passes. Footprint clearance and space for a transfer switch beside the meter are still unmeasured after every hole in the box has closed, so a dense cloud leaves the preview amber on those rules. The useful product change is a single tint and a single imperative while the user is in the scene, then an explicit grade on the review screen that refuses to promote a 20 ft figure into the same bin as a close wall clearance.

The open empirical gap is an outdoor tape of this exact setup, in sun, on siding and a glass meter cover, at one meter and at six. Until that exists, the 1–5 cm band is an upper bound on honesty for close, repeated, high-confidence surfaces, and the thresholds inside the box are knobs for a device afternoon rather than constants from Apple. Glass and deep shadow will keep looking empty; the right response is to say the surface was not measured, and to let the user finish the pass anyway.
