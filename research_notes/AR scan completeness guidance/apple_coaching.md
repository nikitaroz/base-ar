# Apple scan-guidance UX and public APIs

Researched 26 September 2026 from live Apple Developer Documentation JSON, the current Apple sample-code downloads those pages link, the WWDC20 session 10611 page transcript, and the March 2020 iPad Pro newsroom post. Documentation platform tags below are what those pages reported on that date.

## What does ARCoachingOverlayView coach, what goals exist, and what does it not do for mesh completeness?

### Takeaway
`ARCoachingOverlayView` (iOS 13) is a system onboarding overlay for world tracking, finding one plane, relocalization, and geo-tracking. It hides when that goal is met. It has no goal, callback, or progress value for LiDAR mesh or scene-depth coverage.

### Cited Findings
- The view “displays standardized onboarding instructions to direct users toward a specific goal.” It can show automatically during session initialization and in limited tracking, with instructions meant to help ARKit’s world tracking. Illustrations tell the user to start moving the device, keep moving, or change speed. After the overlay decides the goal is met and no further coaching is required, it hides. — [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)
- `activatesAutomatically` defaults to enabled. The overlay then sets itself active when the session is initializing or when tracking has degraded past a threshold. Apps should override `isActive` (via the delegate) and hide unrelated UI while coaching is up. — [activatesAutomatically](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/activatesautomatically); [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)
- Delegate: `coachingOverlayViewWillActivate(_:)`, `coachingOverlayViewDidDeactivate(_:)`, and `coachingOverlayViewDidRequestSessionReset(_:)` when the user taps Start Over during relocalization. If the reset callback is not implemented, Start Over resets tracking and removes existing anchors. — [ARCoachingOverlayViewDelegate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayviewdelegate); [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)
- Relocalization coaching appears when relocalization is enabled and an interruption degrades tracking. The overlay includes a Start Over button so the user can refuse to restore the session. — [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)
- `Goal` cases documented on the enum page:
  - `.tracking` (iOS 13): overlay stays until the user has moved the device enough for ARKit to start basic world tracking.
  - `.horizontalPlane` (iOS 13): stays until ARKit finds at least one horizontal surface.
  - `.verticalPlane` (iOS 13): stays until ARKit finds at least one vertical surface.
  - `.anyPlane` (iOS 13): stays until ARKit finds at least one surface.
  - `.geoTracking`: provides instructions when a geo-tracking session starts or the user goes off course. The case page’s platform metadata says iOS 13.0, while `ARGeoTrackingConfiguration` is documented as introduced in iOS 14 and “occurs exclusively outdoors.”
  — [Goal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum); [tracking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/tracking); [horizontalPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/horizontalplane); [verticalPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/verticalplane); [anyPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/anyplane); [geoTracking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/geotracking); [ARGeoTrackingConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/argeotrackingconfiguration)
- The same Goal page’s topic list, fetched 26 September 2026, contains only those five cases. There is no mesh, scene-reconstruction, or coverage goal. — [Goal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum)
- “Enough” for this overlay is binary and goal-specific: tracking has started, or at least one plane of the requested kind exists, or (for geo) the geo-tracking session is on course. The docs do not define a distance, feature count, or mesh-density threshold. — [tracking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/tracking); [horizontalPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/horizontalplane); [verticalPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/verticalplane); [anyPlane](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/anyplane)
- `ObjectCaptureSession.Tracking` separately says the ARKit coaching overlay appears automatically when object-capture camera tracking is not `.normal`, and the app may need to hide its own UI then. That is tracking coaching during Object Capture, not mesh coaching. — [ObjectCaptureSession.Tracking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/tracking)
- WWDC19 session 604 is titled “Introducing ARKit 3,” the release that matches the iOS 13 coaching API. This research did not extract a coaching transcript from that page. — [WWDC19 session 604](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/604/)

### Inferences
- A third-party app can drop `ARCoachingOverlayView` on an `ARView` / `ARSession` and set `.tracking`, `.horizontalPlane`, `.verticalPlane`, or `.anyPlane` without writing its own move-the-phone animation. That is the reusable piece for “move the iPhone until tracking or a ground/wall plane exists.”
- Plane goals complete when one plane exists. They do not say the wall-plus-ground region around a meter has been scanned.

### Gaps
- The `.geoTracking` case page reports `introducedAt` iOS 13.0 even though geo tracking is an iOS 14 configuration. The doc JSON does not explain that mismatch.
- Apple does not publish the overlay’s exact on-screen sentences as API strings. The overview describes illustrations (start moving, keep moving, change speed), not a localizable enum.
- No source reviewed here says the overlay reacts to `sceneReconstruction` or `ARMeshAnchor` updates.

## How do RoomPlan, Object Capture / Guided Capture, and later Apple scan flows instruct the user, and what completion criteria are public?

### Takeaway
RoomPlan (iOS 16) and Object Capture (iOS 17, area-mode sample in the iOS 18 / WWDC24 sample) expose public coaching signals, but they are instructions for those capture pipelines. None of them is a mesh-completeness API for `ARMeshAnchor`. As of the live enums fetched on 26 September 2026, neither `RoomCaptureSession.Instruction` nor `ObjectCaptureSession.Feedback` has grown a “this mesh region is incomplete” or “point the phone at this world point” case.

### Cited Findings
- RoomPlan “create[s] a 3D model of an interior room” using the camera, LiDAR, trained models, and RealityKit. `RoomCaptureView` shows the camera feed, graphic overlays on physical structures for scan progress, and instructions when a specific movement or perspective is required. When the app determines the scan is complete, the view shows a small-scale room for the user to approve. A custom UI can use `RoomCaptureSession` instead. — [RoomPlan](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan); [RoomCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcaptureview)
- `RoomCaptureSession` “provides instructions that you display to the user.” The delegate method `captureSession(_:didProvide:)` delivers `RoomCaptureSession.Instruction` “to display to the user, such as by presenting the recommendation in a textual label.” — [RoomCaptureSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession); [captureSession(_:didProvide:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesessiondelegate/capturesession(_:didprovide:))
- `Instruction` cases, all marked iOS 16, and the full topic list on 26 September 2026:
  - `.normal` — scanning proceeds normally; the user needs no coaching.
  - `.moveCloseToWall` — move closer to the wall.
  - `.moveAwayFromWall` — move further from the wall.
  - `.slowDown` — move slower.
  - `.turnOnLight` — increase the light in the room.
  - `.lowTexture` — no distinguishable room features. Documented triggers: a solid-color wall, or a viewfinder with no wall edges or other defining shapes.
  — [Instruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction); [normal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/normal); [moveCloseToWall](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/moveclosetowall); [moveAwayFromWall](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/moveawayfromwall); [slowDown](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/slowdown); [turnOnLight](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/turnonlight); [lowTexture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/lowtexture)
- Those cases are semantic recommendations, not a published string catalog. The current RoomPlan sample (commit dated 16 October 2025, “Export CapturedRoom as mesh”) uses `RoomCaptureView` and does not implement `didProvide` or map instructions to custom text, so the built-in view owns the on-screen wording. — [Create a 3D model of an interior room](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/create-a-3d-model-of-an-interior-room-by-guiding-the-user-through-an-ar-experience); sample zip linked from that page
- RoomPlan “enough”: the framework-provided view decides the scan is complete and asks the user to approve a small-scale model. `.normal` means “no coaching right now,” not “the room is finished.” No public completeness percentage or unscanned-region coordinate was on the Instruction or session pages. `RoomCaptureSession.isSupported` is true only if the device has a LiDAR scanner. The sample page says to run on a device with iOS 16 or later and a LiDAR scanner, and associates the sample with a WWDC22 session. — [RoomCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcaptureview); [isSupported](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/issupported); [Create a 3D model of an interior room](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/create-a-3d-model-of-an-interior-room-by-guiding-the-user-through-an-ar-experience)
- Confirmed session titles: WWDC22 session 10127 “Create parametric 3D room scans with RoomPlan”; WWDC23 session 10192 “Explore enhancements to RoomPlan.” This research did not extract new instruction-case names from those transcripts. The live `Instruction` enum still lists the six iOS 16 cases. — [WWDC22 10127](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10127/); [WWDC23 10192](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10192/); [Instruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction)
- Object Capture on device is `ObjectCaptureSession` (iOS 17). `ObjectCaptureView` is “the primary view of the Object Capture front-end 3D UI” and “presents the current state of the wrapped session.” `ObjectCapturePointCloudView` renders the session point cloud, has a turntable gesture, and can `showShotLocations(_:)`. — [ObjectCaptureSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession); [ObjectCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcaptureview); [ObjectCapturePointCloudView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturepointcloudview)
- `Feedback` is a `Set` of simultaneous states (iOS 17 unless noted). Live cases on 26 September 2026:
  - `.objectTooFar` — camera too far to capture the object well.
  - `.objectTooClose` — camera too close to track the object well.
  - `.environmentTooDark` — too dark to proceed; auto-capture stops until lighting improves.
  - `.environmentLowLight` — low light can degrade reconstruction; auto-capture still proceeds.
  - `.movingTooFast` — moving too quickly for clear images; capturing may pause.
  - `.outOfFieldOfView` — object bounding box is outside the camera view; auto-capture will not operate.
  - `.objectNotFlippable` — flipping is unlikely to stitch, usually because the object is featureless or low-texture; multiple height passes without flipping are recommended instead.
  - `.objectNotDetected` (iOS 17.4) — automatic detection failed and a manual box is shown.
  - `.overCapturing` — only if `isOverCaptureEnabled` is true; extra shots will not be used for on-device reconstruction.
  — [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum); case pages linked from that enum
- `userCompletedScanPass` starts false and becomes true “when the user has moved the device in a full circular scan pass around the bounding box of the target object and captured enough data to fill completely the capture dial.” It resets when `beginNewScanPass()` or `beginNewScanPassAfterFlip()` starts another pass. `beginNewScanPass()` is for another orbit at a different height without flipping, and “is only valid in object-centric scanning.” `beginNewScanPassAfterFlip()` returns to detecting so the user picks a new box after flipping. — [userCompletedScanPass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/usercompletedscanpass); [beginNewScanPass()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/beginnewscanpass()); [beginNewScanPassAfterFlip()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/beginnewscanpassafterflip())
- Capture states: `initializing`, `ready`, `detecting`, `capturing`, `finishing`, `completed`, `failed`. The session is over at `completed` or `failed`. — [CaptureState](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/capturestate)
- `isAutoCaptureEnabled` (iOS 18) turns the auto-capture system on or off; if disabled, only manual shots are taken. `maximumNumberOfInputImages` (iOS 17) is the on-device reconstruction cap; capture stops at that cap unless over-capture is enabled. — [isAutoCaptureEnabled](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/isautocaptureenabled); [maximumNumberOfInputImages](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/maximumnumberofinputimages); [isOverCaptureEnabled](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/configuration-swift.struct/isovercaptureenabled)
- Apple’s “Scanning objects using Object Capture” sample is tied to WWDC24 session 10107 and WWDC23 session 10191, requires a LiDAR scanner, an A14 or later, and iOS or iPadOS 18 or later, and the downloaded snapshot is “Updated for Xcode 16 beta 6” on 12 August 2024. WWDC24 session 10107’s page title is “Discover area mode for Object Capture.” — [Scanning objects using Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/scanning-objects-using-object-capture); [WWDC24 10107](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10107/); [WWDC23 10191](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10191/)
- That sample’s user-facing strings are app copy, not framework API strings. Feedback mapping in `FeedbackMessages.swift`:
  - `.objectTooFar` → “Move Closer” (suppressed in the sample’s area mode)
  - `.objectTooClose` → “Move Farther Away” (suppressed in area mode)
  - `.environmentTooDark` → “More Light Required”
  - `.environmentLowLight` → “More Light Recommended”
  - `.movingTooFast` → “Move slower”
  - `.outOfFieldOfView` → “Aim at your object”
  - other cases return nil
  Object-mode guidance also includes “Move slowly around your object.”, “Move close and center the dot on your object, then tap Continue.”, “Move around to ensure that the whole object is inside the box. Drag handles to manually resize.”, and “Can’t find your object. It should be larger than 3 in (8 cm) in each dimension.” Area-mode ready-state copy is “Look at your subject.” Review copy for area mode: “Points show what will be included in the final model. Continue capturing to include a larger area or process now.” — sample `FeedbackMessages.swift`, `CaptureOverlayView.swift`, `OnboardingTutorialView+LocalizedString.swift` inside the zip from [Scanning objects using Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/scanning-objects-using-object-capture)
- The sample’s own `CaptureMode` (`.object` / `.area`) is application state, not an `ObjectCaptureSession` enum found in the documentation pages fetched. Object mode calls `startDetecting()`. Area mode calls `startCapturing()` directly and hides the object reticle with `hideObjectReticle`. Reconstruction sets `PhotogrammetrySession.Configuration.isObjectMaskingEnabled = false` in area mode so the whole image is reconstructed rather than a masked foreground object. Help copy: object mode “works best in controlled indoor environments”; area mode “capture[s] objects and their surroundings” and “works in indoor or outdoor environments.” — sample `BottomOverlayButtons.swift`, `CapturePrimaryView.swift`, `AppDataModel.swift`, `HelpPageView.swift`; [isObjectMaskingEnabled](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/photogrammetrysession/configuration-swift.struct/isobjectmaskingenabled); [hideObjectReticle(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcaptureview)
- Sample completion rules that are not framework constants: `minNumImages = 10` before an object-mode segment is treated as ready to advance; three orbits are “recommended” in the sample’s review strings; the user may skip with a warning that the model may have missing areas. Area mode does not wait on `userCompletedScanPass`; the review state is always “capture in area mode,” and the user chooses to keep capturing or process. — sample `AppDataModel.swift`, `OnboardingTutorialView+LocalizedString.swift`
- Photogrammetry photo guidance (separate from the live guided-capture UI): static objects, substantial overlap (70% or more between adjacent shots; less than 50% may fail), avoid thin, reflective, transparent, or textureless objects, and outdoors “keep the sun out of your images” because hard light including direct sunlight casts shadows that confuse photogrammetry. If images contain depth, RealityKit uses it for real-world scale. — [Capturing photographs for RealityKit Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/capturing-photographs-for-realitykit-object-capture)
- `ObjectCaptureSession.isSupported` must be checked first; creating a session when it is false is a runtime error. The property’s discussion does not itself say “LiDAR.” The iOS 18 sample readme does require a LiDAR scanner. — [isSupported](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/issupported); [Scanning objects using Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/scanning-objects-using-object-capture)
- iOS 26 / 2025–2026: the Feedback and Instruction enumerations fetched on 26 September 2026 do not list extra cases beyond those above. `https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/10107/` returned a generic “WWDC25 - Videos” title and did not mention Object Capture. No public iOS 26 scan-guidance API was found in the pages retrieved.

### Inferences
- The phrases a product team would copy (“Move Closer”, “Move slower”, “Move closer to the wall”) are either RoomPlan instruction meanings or Object Capture sample strings. Reusing the system view (`RoomCaptureView` or `ObjectCaptureView`) gets Apple’s UI only inside that framework’s session, not painted on an arbitrary ARKit scene-reconstruction mesh.
- Object-mode “enough” is the capture dial (`userCompletedScanPass`) plus however many extra orbits the app asks for. Area-mode “enough,” in Apple’s sample, is a human looking at the point cloud. Neither publishes a per-region hole list.

### Gaps
- The exact localized sentences inside `RoomCaptureView` and `ObjectCaptureView` (as opposed to the sample’s overlay strings) are not in the API reference.
- WWDC23 10192 and WWDC24 10107 transcripts were not fully extracted, so any verbal demo wording beyond the documented enums and the August 2024 sample is not recorded here.
- No exhaustive pass of WWDC25 or WWDC26 session lists was completed. Absence of new cases in the live enums is the evidence used for “no new public instruction type,” not a claim that no WWDC talk discussed scanning.

## Which flows are indoor-room, object-orbit, or world-tracking, and which apply to an outdoor wall-plus-ground site survey?

### Takeaway
Coaching-overlay plane and tracking goals are general world tracking and are the only Apple guidance UI that matches an outdoor ARKit placement session on both LiDAR and non-LiDAR phones. RoomPlan is an interior-room scanner. Object Capture object mode is an orbit around a bounded object. Object Capture area mode is the closest outdoor “scan this surroundings” flow, and it is a separate photogrammetry session, not `ARWorldTrackingConfiguration.sceneReconstruction`.

### Cited Findings
- Coaching `.tracking` / plane goals are defined in terms of ARKit world tracking and detected surfaces, not a room model or an object bounding box. `.horizontalPlane` and `.verticalPlane` map directly onto ground and wall planes. — [Goal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum)
- `.geoTracking` coaches outdoor geographic alignment (latitude/longitude/altitude, map data, compass). `ARGeoTrackingConfiguration` “occurs exclusively outdoors.” That is placing content on the globe, not reconstructing a wall mesh. — [geoTracking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum/geotracking); [ARGeoTrackingConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/argeotrackingconfiguration)
- RoomPlan’s own abstract limits the framework to “a 3D model of an interior room” and to identifying walls, windows, openings, doors, and indoor objects such as a fireplace, bed, or refrigerator. Instructions refer to “the wall” and “the room.” — [RoomPlan](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan); [lowTexture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/lowtexture); [turnOnLight](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/turnonlight)
- Object-mode capture is object-centric: a bounding box, a circular pass around that box, optional flip, and `beginNewScanPass()` documented as “only valid in object-centric scanning.” The sample help text says this mode works best indoors. — [beginNewScanPass()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/beginnewscanpass()); [userCompletedScanPass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/usercompletedscanpass); sample `HelpPageView.swift`
- Area mode, as implemented in Apple’s WWDC24 sample, skips object detection, hides the reticle, and is described as capturing surroundings indoors or outdoors. Completion is “continue capturing to include a larger area or process now,” judged from the point cloud. — sample `HelpPageView.swift`, `OnboardingTutorialView+LocalizedString.swift`, `BottomOverlayButtons.swift`; [WWDC24 10107](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10107/)
- Scene reconstruction is a world-tracking configuration flag that yields a polygonal mesh of “the physical environment” / “the real-world surrounding the user,” not a room-plan or an object turntable. WWDC20 session 10611 demonstrates it on a living room (“the entire visible room is meshed”) and says the mesh is aggregated from per-frame LiDAR depth. The API itself is not documented as indoor-only. — [sceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/scenereconstruction); [ARConfiguration.SceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/scenereconstruction); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- Devices without LiDAR: `supportsSceneReconstruction` requires a LiDAR scanner; scene depth is supported only on LiDAR-capable devices; RoomPlan `isSupported` is true only with LiDAR. Plane detection and the coaching overlay are separate world-tracking features and are not gated by that LiDAR check in those pages. — [supportsSceneReconstruction(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/supportsscenereconstruction(_:)); [sceneDepth frame semantic](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/framesemantics-swift.struct/scenedepth); [RoomCaptureSession.isSupported](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/issupported); [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)

### Inferences
- For an outdoor meter-and-wall placement session that must run without LiDAR, the reusable Apple UX is `ARCoachingOverlayView` with `.tracking` plus `.horizontalPlane` or `.verticalPlane` (or `.anyPlane`), plus the app’s own copy driven by `ARCamera.TrackingState`. Mesh completeness coaching cannot come from Apple’s overlay because the mesh will not exist.
- RoomPlan’s “move closer to the wall / move away / slow down / low texture” is the nearest semantic match to a wall survey, but adopting it means running an interior room-capture session, which the framework does not describe as an outdoor site scan.
- Object Capture area mode is the nearest “walk around and the points show what will be included” outdoor flow. It replaces the AR session with an image-capture session aimed at a photogrammetry model. It does not feed `ARMeshAnchor`s back to a placement scene.

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish a matrix that says RoomPlan or scene reconstruction is unsupported outdoors. The indoor limitation for RoomPlan is the product definition (“interior room”), not a documented outdoor failure mode.
- Whether area mode remains sample-level (no framework `CaptureMode`) on later OS releases was not re-checked against an SDK header, only against the August 2024 sample and the Object Capture docs fetched in September 2026, which did not list a capture-mode enum.

## What ARKit properties expose capture quality or coverage, and what do the confidence values mean?

### Takeaway
Public signals are tracking quality, world-map readiness, per-pixel LiDAR depth confidence, and a mesh that updates as ARKit refines the scene. None of them is a coverage score. Confidence is only `low`, `medium`, or `high` per depth pixel. World-mapping status is about saving a relocalizable map, not about mesh accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- `ARCamera.TrackingState`: `.normal` (“optimal results”), `.notAvailable`, or `.limited(Reason)`. In `.limited`, anchor positions “may not be accurate or consistent,” and the reason is “information you can present to the user for improving tracking quality.” Reasons: `.initializing` (not enough camera or motion data yet; temporary after start or configuration changes), `.excessiveMotion` (device moving too fast for image-based tracking), `.insufficientFeatures` (the visible scene lacks distinguishable features), `.relocalizing` (resuming after an interruption; hit tests and anchor placement are less accurate until it returns to `.normal`). — [TrackingState](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum); [limited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/limited(_:)); [normal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/normal); [initializing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/initializing); [excessiveMotion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/excessivemotion); [insufficientFeatures](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/insufficientfeatures); [relocalizing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/relocalizing)
- `ARFrame.worldMappingStatus` is “whether the session currently has enough data to generate a useful `ARWorldMap`,” not a mesh-quality flag. Cases (iOS 12): `.notAvailable` (no internal map; saving fails; typical just after start), `.limited` (space around the device is not sufficiently mapped; a saved map is unlikely to relocalize well), `.extending` (recently visited areas are mapped at high fidelity, but the current viewpoint is still being mapped; moderate to high relocalization reliability), `.mapped` (high-fidelity map of the space around the current position and the visible scene; highest relocalization reliability if the later device is in a similar pose). — [worldMappingStatus](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.property); [notAvailable](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.enum/notavailable); [limited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.enum/limited); [extending](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.enum/extending); [mapped](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/worldmappingstatus-swift.enum/mapped)
- Scene depth: add `.sceneDepth` or `.smoothedSceneDepth` to frame semantics. Both are nil by default. `.sceneDepth` is the LiDAR reading for that frame; `.smoothedSceneDepth` averages across frames to reduce flicker. Check support with `supportsFrameSemantics`; the discussion says scene depth returns true on devices that support the LiDAR scanner’s depth buffer, and says not to call it on the `ARConfiguration` superclass. — [sceneDepth property](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/scenedepth); [smoothedSceneDepth property](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/smoothedscenedepth); [sceneDepth semantic](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/framesemantics-swift.struct/scenedepth); [smoothedSceneDepth semantic](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/framesemantics-swift.struct/smoothedscenedepth); [supportsFrameSemantics(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/supportsframesemantics(_:))
- `ARDepthData.depthMap` is distance in meters from the plane of the camera. `confidenceMap` is a pixel buffer of `ARConfidenceLevel` raw values, one per depth sample, “useful in filtering out lower-accuracy depth values.” “The natural light of the physical environment affects” depth “such that ARKit is less confident about the accuracy of the LiDAR Scanner’s depth measurements for surfaces that are highly reflective, or that have high light absorption.” — [ARDepthData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata); [depthMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/depthmap); [confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap)
- `ARConfidenceLevel` (iOS 14): `.low` “less confident,” `.medium` “moderately confident,” `.high` “fairly confident.” WWDC20 session 10611 says the same three levels exist so apps can filter depth, and that they correspond per depth pixel because LiDAR depth depends on reflected light. The documentation declarations fetched do not print numeric raw values. — [ARConfidenceLevel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel); [low](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel/low); [medium](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel/medium); [high](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel/high); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- The Depth Cloud sample places one point per depth sample for a single frame and shows a dark gap “where the current sensor readings lack color and depth information.” It also lets the user cycle confidence, enlarge the depth buffer, and toggle smoothed depth. WWDC20 session 10611 “references a prior version of this sample app that accumulates points.” The current sample page describes a per-frame cloud, not an accumulated coverage volume. — [Displaying a point cloud using scene depth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth)
- Scene reconstruction (documented availability iOS 13.4, which is when the LiDAR iPad Pro API shipped): `.mesh` is a polygonal approximation; `.meshWithClassification` adds classification of real-world objects. Call `supportsSceneReconstruction` first. “Scene reconstruction requires a device with a LiDAR Scanner.” Enabling plane detection makes ARKit smooth the mesh where it detects a plane. Enabling people occlusion removes mesh that overlaps detected people. — [sceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/scenereconstruction); [mesh](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/scenereconstruction/mesh); [meshWithClassification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/scenereconstruction/meshwithclassification); [supportsSceneReconstruction(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/supportsscenereconstruction(_:))
- `ARMeshAnchor`: the scene is subdivided into mesh anchors that “constantly update their data as ARKit refines its understanding of the real world.” A later mesh change after the physical scene changes “is not intended to reflect in real time.” — [ARMeshAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshanchor)
- `ARMeshGeometry` stores vertices, faces, normals, and an optional per-face `classification`. Unclassified faces use `ARMeshClassification.none`. Classification cases listed: `ceiling`, `door`, `floor`, `none`, `seat`, `table`, `wall`, `window`. There is no “incomplete,” “unscanned,” or confidence channel on the geometry type. — [ARMeshGeometry](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshgeometry); [classification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshgeometry/classification); [ARMeshClassification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification); [none](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification/none); [wall](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification/wall); [floor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification/floor)
- WWDC20 session 10611: scene geometry is built by aggregating depth “across multiple frames”; depth is exposed at 60 Hz with each AR frame; triangles “vary in size to show the optimum detail for each surface”; with classification, the demo colors seats and floor differently. The transcript’s “5 meters” hits are the unrelated “35 meters” sample code, not a range spec. — [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- No symbol named `lidarAvailability` exists at the ARKit configuration, `ARSession`, or `AVCaptureDevice` documentation paths probed (HTTP 404 on 26 September 2026). The documented device checks are `supportsSceneReconstruction(_:)`, `supportsFrameSemantics(_:)`, `RoomCaptureSession.isSupported`, and `ObjectCaptureSession.isSupported`. — those documentation URLs above

### Inferences
- A scene-reconstruction point cloud can treat missing mesh in a volume the user cares about, or a cluster of `.low` depth pixels, as “not good enough,” but that rule is the app’s. Apple does not define the cutoff.
- `worldMappingStatus == .mapped` should not be shown as “the site scan is accurate.” It means the visible area is a good candidate for a saved world map.
- `.insufficientFeatures` and RoomPlan `.lowTexture` are the same kind of user problem (blank wall, weak texture) reported by different stacks. Only the tracking-state reason is available in a plain ARKit session without RoomPlan.
- Without LiDAR, the observable quality signals that still exist are `trackingState`, plane anchors, and `worldMappingStatus`. `sceneDepth`, `smoothedSceneDepth`, and mesh anchors are absent because the configuration will not enable them.

### Gaps
- Numeric raw values for `ARConfidenceLevel` (often assumed 0/1/2 in sample code elsewhere) are not written in the documentation pages fetched. Do not treat a specific integer as documented from this pass.
- The pages do not say whether `ARMeshGeometry.classification` is nil when reconstruction is `.mesh` rather than `.meshWithClassification`. The property type is optional; the prose only defines `.none` as “can’t classify.”
- No documented metric converts confidence or triangle size into survey accuracy in inches.

## Is there a public API that says a mesh region is incomplete or that tells the user where to point the phone?

### Takeaway
No. For ARKit scene reconstruction the developer infers gaps from the mesh and the depth buffer. The only public “point the phone this way” APIs are coaching goals, tracking-state reasons, RoomPlan’s six instructions, and Object Capture feedback, and none of those returns a world-space target on the mesh.

### Cited Findings
- Coaching completion is “goal met,” then the view hides. Goals are tracking, one plane, or geo-tracking. — [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview); [Goal](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview/goal-swift.enum)
- RoomPlan’s `didProvide` instruction is an enum value the app may put in a label (`moveCloseToWall`, and the other five). It is not a ray, a bounding box, or an unscanned mesh face. — [captureSession(_:didProvide:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesessiondelegate/capturesession(_:didprovide:))
- Object Capture feedback describes the object box, distance, lighting, speed, and field of view. `outOfFieldOfView` means the box is outside the camera, which is the closest thing to “aim over there,” and it is about the object bounds, not an incomplete patch of an `ARMeshAnchor`. Area mode in the sample suppresses the move-closer / move-farther strings and tells the user to look at the subject, then judge the point cloud. — [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum); [outOfFieldOfView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum/outoffieldofview); sample `FeedbackMessages.swift`
- Mesh anchors expose geometry and optional classification only. Classification `.none` means ARKit cannot classify that face, not that the face is missing. — [ARMeshAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshanchor); [ARMeshClassification.none](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification/none)
- A single depth frame can simply lack samples (Depth Cloud’s dark gap behind the object). Session 10611 describes the mesh as a later aggregation of those frames, still without a hole-query API. — [Displaying a point cloud using scene depth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)

### Inferences
- An app that only uses scene reconstruction has to invent the cue: for example, compare mesh faces or high-confidence depth points against the ground plane and wall plane near the meter, then show its own “step back” or “look along the wall” text. Apple does not supply that cue.
- Copying RoomPlan or Object Capture strings into that UI would be mimicking their wording. The enums will not fire unless the app is actually running those sessions.

### Gaps
- Private or undocumented scanning UI inside `RoomCaptureView` and `ObjectCaptureView` may highlight regions visually. That behavior is not a documented callback an `ARSession` observer can read. It is labeled here as undocumented, not as an API.

## What are the practical outdoor limits of LiDAR reconstruction?

### Takeaway
Apple’s March 2020 LiDAR announcement says the scanner measures objects up to 5 meters away and works indoors and outdoors. ARKit then lowers depth confidence on reflective and high-absorption surfaces, fuses depth into a mesh that lags real-world changes, and will not produce scene depth or a reconstruction mesh at all without LiDAR. No fetched document says ARKit hides reconstruction in sunlight.

### Cited Findings
- “The LiDAR Scanner measures the distance to surrounding objects up to 5 meters away, works both indoors and outdoors, and operates at the photon level at nano-second speeds.” That sentence is from the 18 March 2020 iPad Pro announcement, which also says new depth frameworks in iPadOS combine LiDAR depth points with cameras and motion sensors. — [Apple newsroom, 18 March 2020](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2020/03/apple-unveils-new-ipad-pro-with-lidar-scanner-and-trackpad-support-in-ipados/)
- The 13 October 2020 iPhone 12 Pro newsroom post describes a LiDAR scanner for AR and does not, in the LiDAR paragraphs extracted, repeat the “5 meters” sentence. WWDC20 session 10611’s transcript does not state a meter range for the scanner (the only “5 meters” substring is inside “35 meters” of unrelated sample code). — [iPhone 12 Pro newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2020/10/apple-introduces-iphone-12-pro-and-iphone-12-pro-max-with-5g/); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- Depth confidence drops when surfaces are highly reflective or highly light-absorbing. The confidence-map discussion attributes that to “the natural light of the physical environment.” Session 10611 ties the same limitation to LiDAR depending on light reflected from objects. Neither source says the mesh is disabled in sun. — [confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- Direct sunlight is a documented problem for Object Capture photographs (hard shadows confuse photogrammetry; keep the sun out of the frames), not for the scene-reconstruction mesh. — [Capturing photographs for RealityKit Object Capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/capturing-photographs-for-realitykit-object-capture)
- Mesh update behavior: anchors continually refine, but a mesh edit after a physical change is “not intended to reflect in real time.” Plane detection, if also on, smooths mesh on detected planes. People occlusion punches people out of the mesh. Triangles vary in size for “optimum detail” (WWDC20 demo of a living room, not an outdoor wall). — [ARMeshAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshanchor); [sceneReconstruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/scenereconstruction); [WWDC20 session 10611](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10611/)
- Reconstruction and scene depth are unavailable when `supportsSceneReconstruction` / `supportsFrameSemantics(.sceneDepth)` is false. The documented reason is lack of a LiDAR scanner. The reconstructed-scene sample describes the behavior on a fourth-generation iPad Pro running iPadOS 13.4 or later: LiDAR depth becomes vertices, partitioned across multiple mesh anchors. — [supportsSceneReconstruction(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/supportsscenereconstruction(_:)); [Visualizing and interacting with a reconstructed scene](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/visualizing-and-interacting-with-a-reconstructed-scene)
- Outdoor world tracking can still fail closed for non-LiDAR reasons: `.insufficientFeatures` when the camera does not see enough distinguishable features, and `.excessiveMotion` when the phone moves too fast. Those states are what the coaching overlay and any custom banner can respond to on a non-LiDAR phone. — [insufficientFeatures](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/insufficientfeatures); [excessiveMotion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcamera/trackingstate-swift.enum/reason/excessivemotion); [ARCoachingOverlayView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arcoachingoverlayview)
- Classification labels are indoor-object oriented (`wall`, `floor`, `ceiling`, `table`, `seat`, `window`, `door`, `none`). The docs do not define an outdoor ground or sky class, and they do not say a sunlit wall is left unclassified. — [ARMeshClassification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification)

### Inferences
- Beyond about 5 meters, an app should not expect LiDAR samples, because that is the only published scanner range found, and it is a 2020 hardware announcement rather than an ARKit property. Anything closer can still be low-confidence if the surface does not return light well.
- A painted exterior wall can trip `.insufficientFeatures` / low texture even when the phone has LiDAR, because tracking is still image-based. That is a reason to keep the coaching overlay’s tracking goal, independent of mesh density.
- “Green when the cloud is accurate enough” cannot be read from a system flag outdoors. The closest public ingredients are: tracking `.normal`, a horizontal and a vertical plane, mesh faces actually present beside the meter and wall, and depth samples at `.high` rather than `.low`. The combination rule is application logic.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 Apple spec page was found that restates or changes the 5-meter figure for later iPhone LiDAR scanners. Do not treat 5 meters as reconfirmed for every model sold by September 2026.
- No document fetched says ARKit degrades, hides, or refuses scene reconstruction specifically because the session is outdoors or in direct sun. That behavior, if it exists, was not in the public pages reviewed.
- Outdoor classification rates (does a concrete pad come back as `.floor`?) are not documented.
