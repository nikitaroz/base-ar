# Product patterns for scan completeness and rescan prompts

Research date: 26 September 2026. Sources are official help centers, Apple Developer Documentation markdown, and App Store listing text retrieved that day. “The app says you can finish” is kept separate from “the model is accurate.” Marketing accuracy lines are labeled as marketing.

Canvas naming: App Store search for “Canvas by Occupier” and “Occupier Canvas” did not return a scanning app. The construction scanner formerly called Canvas is listed as **Twindo (formerly Canvas)** by Occipital, Inc. (App Store id 1169235377, version 4.15). Support pages still say “Twindo™ (formerly Canvas).” Findings below use Twindo’s current docs.

## What do Polycam, SiteScape, Canvas, Matterport, Magicplan, and similar apps show while scanning, and when do they enable “done”?

### Takeaway
Published UX is a live picture of what has been seen (grid, wall color, checkmark, blur) plus a user-tapped Done or Process. None of the help articles retrieved say the Done button stays disabled until a coverage percentage is met. Apple’s RoomPlan view is the exception in wording: the framework decides the scan is complete enough to show a miniature room for approval, but it does not publish the rule.

### Cited Findings
- **Polycam Space mode (LiDAR), current help.** While scanning, a blue grid marks surfaces not yet captured; it is replaced by white mesh where LiDAR has scanned. If the floor-plan toggle is on, white outlines appear when a wall is detected. Auto shutter is the default and the phone buzzes (haptics) to confirm it is collecting. Manual shutter requires the user to tap for each photo. The user taps a white circle to start and taps **Done** when they decide to finish. Help tells them to tap Done “when the room is fully covered (no blue grid remaining).” It does not say the button is locked until then. After Done, the user picks a processing preset (Default, Dense, Custom, or Cloud). — [How to Use Space Mode with LiDAR-enabled devices](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices)
- **Polycam as-built blog (different color language).** A Polycam blog walkthrough says to “fill in all of the purple areas” and make white lines follow room corners, including ceiling and floor, then click **Process**. It also says that if angles are missing, “Polycam will do its best to guess,” and dimensions and spatial reports will be less accurate. Suggested pace: about two minutes per 100 square feet when accuracy matters; a faster pass is possible “if accuracy and completeness aren’t important.” The blog does not date the UI, so purple-vs-blue may be an older or different mode than the current Space-mode article. — [How to Create As-Built Documentation With Polycam](https://poly.cam/blog/how-to-3d-scan-as-builts-with-polycam)
- **Polycam getting started.** Generic flow: follow the on-screen guide, tap Done, choose a processing type. No completeness meter is described. — [Getting Started with Polycam](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/39279656782996-Getting-Started-with-Polycam)
- **SiteScape (FARO), help on HoloBuilder/Sphere XG.** Scanning advice is a “digital spray can”: even coverage, including nooks, no jerky motion, limit large left-right rotations, keep the device about 3–12 ft (1–4 m) from surfaces. The user guide’s “Capturing a scan” section, as published, does not describe arrows, a heatmap, a checkmark, or a disabled Done control. **Multi-scan** adds a **progress bar**: when it is full, tap capture again to start the next segment (up to 10). That bar is a segment-length limit because position drifts the longer you scan, not a map of holes. Overlap adjacent segments, then merge on the web by lining walls up to a grid. — [Scanning Best Practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices); [Using Multi-Scan](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178760-using-multi-scan); [User Guide](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178758-user-guide)
- **Twindo, formerly Canvas (Occipital).** Room tutorial: start in a detailed corner, stand 5–10 ft back, landscape, scan up and down the wall in one direction “as if you are spraying paint” (“fence painting”), walk the perimeter back to the start, tap **Done**. Do not need the entire ceiling or floor. Tips: do not pivot without continuing the pattern; do not capture the same thing twice; one floor at a time; a “J” pattern in halls and stairs. No live percentage, heatmap, or locked Done is described. The in-app scan can still be viewed, measured, shared, and exported as OBJ even when a later Scan-to-CAD order is rejected. — [How do I scan a room with Twindo?](https://www.twindo.com/support/tutorial-how-to-scan-a-room-with-canvas/) (updated 14 September 2026); [Why couldn’t my scan be processed?](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/)
- **magicplan Manual-Scan, LiDAR Wall mode.** Point at one wall after another. A wall is captured when **it turns green and a green check mark appears**. Blocked walls can fall back to Corner mode (AR, green corner indicator, white grid). Perimeter can end by returning to the first corner or tapping **Done**. Calibration copy: point at your feet, then the ceiling. Help says stay in the same spot for the whole manual scan. — [Scan a Floor Plan with Manual-Scan (iOS)](https://help.magicplan.app/scan-a-room-in-seconds-using-lidar)
- **magicplan LiDAR Auto-Scan (Apple RoomPlan, iOS 17, iPhone 12 Pro / iPad Pro or newer).** Pre-scan tips, then a red record button. On-screen instruction to move the device and point at the top edge of a wall. White lines show wall detection. Optional 2D or 3D floor-plan map while scanning. Photo shutter drops pictures onto the plan. Stop the room with the record button again, then review: **green walls** mean successfully scanned; **orange walls** mean not properly scanned. Buttons: **Confirm Scan** or **Discard & Rescan**. After all rooms, tap **Done**, then **Generate Floor Plan**. Help says Auto-Scan will still try to close a room even if walls were not fully captured. If you confirm an orange wall, that room does not render in the 2D plan. There is no way to hand-close a failed room. — [Scan a Floor Plan with LiDAR Auto-Scan (iOS)](https://help.magicplan.app/auto-scan-your-floor-plan); [LiDAR & Auto-Scan FAQ](https://help.magicplan.app/autoscan-faq)
- **Apple RoomPlan (the API magicplan Auto-Scan says it uses).** `RoomCaptureView` shows the camera, “real-time graphic overlays” on physical structures for progress, and text instructions when a specific movement or view is required. “When the app determines that the current scan is complete, the view displays a small-scale version of the scanned room for the user to approve.” A miniature model at the bottom can be toggled with `isModelEnabled`. Coaching cases the app is told to display: `normal`, `moveCloseToWall`, `moveAwayFromWall`, `turnOnLight`, `slowDown`, `lowTexture`. `lowTexture` fires for a solid-color wall or a view with no wall edges or other defining shapes. Docs do not publish the completeness test behind “determines that the current scan is complete.” — [RoomCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcaptureview); [RoomCaptureSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession); [RoomCaptureSession.Instruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction); [lowTexture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/lowtexture)
- **Apple Object Capture (RealityKit, iOS 17+), the guided object UI third-party object scanners wrap.** `ObjectCaptureView` is “the primary view of the Object Capture front-end 3D UI” and can show an object-selection reticle in the `.ready` state. `ObjectCaptureSession.feedback` is a set of problems, not a percent: `environmentLowLight` (auto-capture continues, quality may suffer), `environmentTooDark` (auto-capture stops until there is more light), `movingTooFast` (capture may pause), `objectTooClose`, `objectTooFar`, `outOfFieldOfView` (bounding box not in frame, auto-capture will not run), `objectNotDetected` (manual box instead), `objectNotFlippable` (do not flip a low-texture object; do extra height passes instead), `overCapturing` (more shots than `maximumNumberOfInputImages`, extra shots unused for on-device reconstruction). The session **stops taking images** at `maximumNumberOfInputImages` unless over-capture is enabled. That number is a runtime property; the docs do not publish a fixed count. `finish()` is something the app requests. `ObjectCapturePointCloudView` is a review of the cloud **before** finishing, with optional shot locations and a turntable gesture. Reconstruction is a separate later phase. — [ObjectCaptureSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession); [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum); [maximumNumberOfInputImages](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/maximumnumberofinputimages); [ObjectCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcaptureview); [ObjectCapturePointCloudView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturepointcloudview)
- **Scaniverse (Niantic Spatial), support page.** During a scan the scene “start[s] by looking blurry and gradually sharpen[s] to indicate where you have captured useful angles.” The user is told to focus on parts that are still blurry. Recommended duration is **1–3 minutes**; “any longer, and the quality may actually decline.” Processing can be now or later. No Done-lock or percentage is described. Support also says splats are the mode if you need to capture **beyond the 5-meter range of the LiDAR scanner** (or their photogrammetry mode); meshes are the mode “if you need precise measurements.” — [How to use Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support)
- **Modelar (App Store text only, version 3.1.2, 2 September 2026).** Store copy: see what you captured while you scan, review on device, measure before leaving the site. No coverage rule in that text. — [Modelar on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/modelar-3d-lidar-scanner/id1572844190)
- **Matterport app listing (version 5.69, 1 September 2026).** Store copy describes smartphone, tablet, 360, or Pro-camera capture and virtual tours. It does not describe a coverage meter, rescan prompt, or Done rule. — [Matterport on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/matterport/id701086043)
- **OpenSpace (jobsite, not a LiDAR mesh coach).** Store copy: mount a 360 camera on a hard hat, hit record, keep working; AI maps video to blueprints afterward. No in-walk “this region is sparse” control is described. — [OpenSpace on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/openspace-ai-construction-app/id1390396977)

### Inferences
- The shared live pattern is “show the unscanned part,” not “block the button.” Polycam’s blue grid, magicplan’s green check vs orange wall, Scaniverse’s blur-to-sharp, and RoomPlan’s structure overlays are the same idea with different drawings.
- “Done” in these products means the user stopped the pass. A later process (Polycam processing presets, Twindo Scan-to-CAD rejection, magicplan dropping an unclosed room from the 2D plan, Object Capture reconstruction) is where incompleteness actually bites.
- magicplan is the clearest published example of a RoomPlan app turning Apple’s coaching and wall state into checkmarks, green/orange, and an explicit rescan choice.
- Object Capture’s UI is an orbit around a bounded object (reticle, field of view, flip, shot locations, turntable review). It is a different task from walking a wall.

### Gaps
- Matterport’s current help center did not return a capture-tips article in this pass (tried `support.matterport.com` article URLs; 404). No citable iPhone-capture coverage UI or Done rule.
- No Occupier-branded Canvas help site was found. If that name is an internal alias, these notes only cover Twindo/Occipital’s public pages.
- Polycam help does not say whether Done is disabled while blue grid remains. Only the recommended moment to tap it is published.
- Apple does not publish the numeric test behind RoomCaptureView’s “scan is complete.”
- `maximumNumberOfInputImages` has no published constant in the page fetched.
- 3D Scanner App, KIRI Engine, and Luma 3D Capture store pages were retrieved, but not help articles that describe their live completeness UI, so they are not treated as evidence.

## Do any publish a coverage threshold, minimum photo count, blur or motion gate, or mesh-density check?

### Takeaway
Published numeric gates exist for **photo and video uploads** and for **motion, light, and distance coaching**. They do not exist, in the sources fetched, as a mesh-density percentage or a “coverage must exceed N%” rule that unlocks Done on a LiDAR room scan.

### Cited Findings
- **Polycam LiDAR Space, manual shutter:** “minimum of 20 images with 70% overlap between consecutive images.” Auto mode has no published image count. Custom processing exposes **Depth Range** (max 5 m, “set by the range of the LiDAR sensor”), **Voxel Size** (smaller = more polygons, longer), and **Simplification** (example: 40% polygon reduction before texturing). Those are processing knobs after capture, not a live pass/fail. Help names failure modes without thresholds: tracking lost from “too much sunlight or moving too fast”; blur from rotating the phone instead of the body; incomplete coverage = blue grid still visible. — [Space Mode (LiDAR)](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices)
- **Polycam cloud photogrammetry / splat upload (not the live LiDAR pass):** “photos overlap by 60–80%”; “anywhere between 20 and 2,000 images”; hard minimum “The minimum number of images is 20.” Video: minimum 15 seconds, maximum 30 minutes or 16 GB. **Smart Keyframing** “automatically select[s] the best non-blurred keyframes” and “filters out blurry or redundant frames.” Plan caps: Free 150 images / 3 min video; Basic 300 / 5 min; Business and Enterprise 2,000 images / 30 min (Gaussian splat cap 1,000 on some plans). — [Photogrammetry and Gaussian splats from images and videos](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/30549121659412-How-to-Create-Photogrammetry-and-Gaussian-splats-from-Existing-Images-and-Videos-on-Mobile-and-Web)
- **SiteScape:** distance band 3–12 ft (1–4 m). No photo count, overlap percent, blur score, or mesh density. Multi-scan cap is **10 segments**, triggered when a progress bar fills; the article does not say what fills the bar (time, distance, or point count). — [Scanning Best Practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices); [Using Multi-Scan](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178760-using-multi-scan)
- **Twindo:** no coverage percent or mesh-density check in the scan tutorial. Practical limits they do publish: try to keep a scan **under about 10 minutes**; device RAM “max out at about one floor of a typical home”; stand **5–10 ft** away to start; LiDAR range **5 m (about 16 ft 5 in)** on the exterior page and **5 m (15 ft)** on the non-residential page (the two pages disagree on the foot conversion). Scan-to-CAD rejection reasons are qualitative: messy/distorted, re-scanning the same area, zig-zag instead of the recommended path, camera covered, too close or too far, poor or no light, moving objects/people/shadows, partial spaces with no boundary, non-room scans. — [How big of a space can I scan?](https://www.twindo.com/support/how-big-of-a-space-can-i-scan/); [Can I scan exteriors?](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/); [Non-residential](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-want-to-scan-non-residential-environments-will-canvas-work-outside-of-the-home/); [Why couldn’t my scan be processed?](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/)
- **Twindo accuracy claim (CAD file, not a live gate):** “most measurements in your CAD file should be within 1–2%” of tape, laser, or an existing blueprint, from “case studies we’ve run with professionals.” They explicitly refuse an absolute tolerance (“all measurements ever will always be within X millimeters”). They say outliers usually come from poor technique, and that small dimensions under about one foot can exceed 1–2% because 1/16 in can be more than 2%. This is about the model they deliver after processing, not about enabling Done. — [What kind of accuracy can I expect from Twindo?](https://www.twindo.com/support/what-kind-of-accuracy-can-i-expect-from-canvas/) (updated 5 February 2026)
- **magicplan:** no published coverage percent, photo minimum, or mesh density. Wall state is binary in the help text (green/check vs orange). Storage advice for drift: at least 10% free storage, or 10 GB on a small phone. The LiDAR explainer says LiDAR can capture “down to the millimeter.” That sentence is product marketing on a concept page, not a measured error or a Done rule. — [Auto-Scan FAQ](https://help.magicplan.app/autoscan-faq); [How Does LiDAR Scanning Work With magicplan?](https://help.magicplan.app/how-does-lidar-scan-work)
- **magicplan “How Accurate is magicplan’s Room Scan?”** exists and its meta description is about the “scan with camera” feature, not a published percent. The article body did not render without JavaScript, so no figure from that page is cited. — [How Accurate is magicplan’s Room Scan?](https://help.magicplan.app/how-accurate-is-magicplan)
- **Scaniverse:** 1–3 minute recommendation; quality “may actually decline” after that. No photo count or density threshold. Blur clearing is the coverage signal. 5 m called out as the LiDAR (and their photogrammetry) range limit versus splats. — [How to use Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support)
- **Apple Object Capture motion and light gates** (behavioral, not a coverage percent): too fast may pause capture; too dark stops auto-capture; low light allows capture but warns; too close, too far, or box out of frame blocks or degrades tracking/auto-capture; shot count above `maximumNumberOfInputImages` is over-capture, a ceiling rather than a minimum. No minimum shot count is listed on the Feedback page. — [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum)
- **Apple RoomPlan motion and scene gates:** `slowDown`, `moveCloseToWall`, `moveAwayFromWall`, `turnOnLight`, `lowTexture`. No distance in meters and no coverage percent on those pages. — [Instruction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction)
- **Apple LiDAR confidence (API, not an app prompt):** `ARFrame.sceneDepth` includes confidence in the estimated distance. `ARDepthData.confidenceMap` stores an `ARConfidenceLevel` raw value per depth pixel: `low`, `medium`, `high`. Apple says confidence drops on highly reflective surfaces and surfaces with high light absorption, and that natural light affects the depth map. This is a per-pixel buffer for the app to read. The docs do not tell a user-facing app to show a percentage or to block Done. Mesh anchors (`ARMeshGeometry`) publish face classification, not this confidence map. — [sceneDepth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arframe/scenedepth); [confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap); [ARConfidenceLevel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfidencelevel); [ARMeshGeometry](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshgeometry)
- **SiteScape App Store (marketing, not a gate):** “Capture 3D scans accurate to the inch” and “inch-level accuracy,” building scale “up to 5,000 ft² or 500 m² with SiteScape Pro.” Version 1.8.4, 11 August 2026. Not a measured study and not a completeness rule. — [SiteScape on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sitescape-lidar-scanner-cad/id1524700432)

### Inferences
- Where a number is published, it is usually an input recipe (20 photos, 60–80% or 70% overlap, 15-second video, 1–4 m standoff, 1–3 or under-10 minutes) or a sensor limit (about 5 m), not a mesh-quality score.
- Blur is handled either by coaching (“slow down,” “don’t spin the phone”) or by dropping frames later (Polycam Smart Keyframing). It is not described as a user-visible blur percentage during LiDAR scanning.
- Apple’s confidence map is the actual per-sample quality signal on LiDAR depth. Shipping apps in this set, in the pages fetched, do not say they surface low/medium/high to the user.

### Gaps
- No fetched page states a mesh-density or point-spacing threshold (for example points per square inch) that must pass before finish.
- SiteScape does not define what fills the multi-scan progress bar.
- Matterport overlap distances and “scan too far from the last position” rules were not retrieved from a current help article. Older Pro-camera practice is not cited here because the page was not opened.
- magicplan’s accuracy article body was not readable without JavaScript.
- Object Capture’s maximum image count is device-reported and unpublished as a constant.

## How do jobsite or as-built apps warn that a region is sparse versus letting the user finish a pretty but incomplete cloud?

### Takeaway
Two patterns show up. Polycam and magicplan warn **during** the scan (unfilled grid, or orange walls that will not become a room). Twindo and SiteScape mostly **let the capture finish**, then either ask a person to reject it (Twindo Scan-to-CAD) or leave hole-filling to the user’s eye and a later merge (SiteScape). Several products will still produce a model by guessing or auto-closing.

### Cited Findings
- **Polycam, live hole marker.** Blue grid (help) or purple areas (blog) stay visible until scanned. Checklist before finishing: no blue grid left, white wall outlines if floor plan is on, corners captured. The blog states the failure mode of finishing early: the app “will do its best to guess what you haven’t captured,” and “the visuals will be off and both the dimensions and the auto-generated spatial reports will be less accurate.” Help also says **do not** make a second pass over areas already captured: “Redundant passes can introduce artifacts and reduce model quality,” and “Scan each area only once.” So the published fix for a hole is to keep going until the grid clears, not to scrub the same patch repeatedly. — [Space Mode (LiDAR)](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices); [As-built blog](https://poly.cam/blog/how-to-3d-scan-as-builts-with-polycam)
- **magicplan, explicit sparse wall.** Orange wall = not properly scanned. Confirming it means that room is missing from the 2D sketch. The app may auto-close a partial room “if there is enough data”; otherwise discard and rescan. There is no manual close. Open floor plans, glass, stairwells, and heavy construction are called out as cases to switch to Manual-Scan. Doors should be closed so adjoining rooms are not swallowed. This is a warning that a pretty scan can still omit a room, and the warning is the orange color plus a missing room after generate — not a density heatmap. — [Auto-Scan](https://help.magicplan.app/auto-scan-your-floor-plan); [Auto-Scan FAQ](https://help.magicplan.app/autoscan-faq)
- **Twindo, finish now, reject later.** The room tutorial’s Done is after the user has walked the perimeter. Nothing in that article blocks a sparse cloud. Scan-to-CAD later rejects some scans and asks for a rescan, without charging: poor quality (including re-scanning the same area, zig-zag, too close/far, dark, motion), **partial spaces** (“scans of open spaces”; they need a boundary such as a wall, window, or door to know where the model starts and stops), and non-room objects. “There is nothing within the app that will prevent you from scanning” non-residential spaces. Exterior single walls are accepted for 3D models; price then uses wall area instead of closed floor area. So an open wall can be a valid capture for them, and a closed-room assumption is a CAD-pipeline rule, not a live scanner lock. — [Room tutorial](https://www.twindo.com/support/tutorial-how-to-scan-a-room-with-canvas/); [Rejection](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/); [Exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/); [Non-residential](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-want-to-scan-non-residential-environments-will-canvas-work-outside-of-the-home/)
- **SiteScape, no hole alarm in the guide.** The spray-can metaphor is the only coverage instruction. Multi-scan says “avoid gaps” by overlapping segments, then a person aligns walls on the desktop. A sparse cloud can be synced. The progress bar ending a segment is about drift, not holes. — [Best practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices); [Multi-scan](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178760-using-multi-scan)
- **Scaniverse.** Blurry regions are the sparse signal, and the instruction is to keep moving until they sharpen. No article says the app refuses to process a blurry scan. Support says the user chooses mesh vs splat after the fact, including reprocess. — [How to use Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support)
- **OpenSpace.** Capture is “hit record and go” while working. Completeness is whatever path the wearer walked; the listing does not describe a sparse-region warning. — [OpenSpace on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/openspace-ai-construction-app/id1390396977)
- **Apple RoomPlan.** Overlays show progress on structures. Completeness is an internal decision that leads to a miniature for approval. magicplan’s orange-wall behavior is a third-party reading of that kind of session, not a number in Apple’s docs. — [RoomCaptureView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcaptureview)

### Inferences
- “Pretty but incomplete” is a known failure these vendors describe in words (Polycam will guess; magicplan will try to close the room; Twindo will sometimes reject only after upload). The products that show it live use color on the missing surface, not a single percent.
- A second pass is not universally recommended. Polycam and Twindo both say extra passes over the same area can hurt the model. The useful prompt is “this patch is still empty,” not “scrub it again.”
- Jobsite tools aimed at whole buildings (SiteScape multi-scan, OpenSpace) optimize for not losing tracking over a long walk. They do not try to prove every square foot before the user leaves.

### Gaps
- No fetched jobsite app publishes a heatmap legend (for example red = too few points) with a numeric cutoff.
- Matterport’s dollhouse “missing area” behavior was not verified from a current article.
- Twindo does not publish the in-app viewer’s colors, if any, for low-confidence mesh. Rejection copy is post-upload email, not a screenshot of a live warning.

## Which patterns are realistic outdoors in daylight with a LiDAR iPhone (about 5 m, no turntable, user on the ground)?

### Takeaway
A short walk along a wall, staying roughly 1–4 m off it, with up-and-down passes and a “still missing” mark on that wall and the ground in front of it, matches what these vendors already tell people to do outside or on a single surface. Orbiting an object, closing a room, and trusting data toward the sun or past about 5 m do not.

### Cited Findings
- **Range about 5 m is the vendors’ own number, not only Apple’s marketing.** Polycam: custom Depth Range max “5m is set by the range of the LiDAR sensor”; restricting it can drop background. Twindo exteriors: “The LiDAR sensor’s range is 5m or about 16'5"”; from the ground, “you could capture about 20–22' okay, but accuracy will decline as you approach the maximum end of the range, and beyond that will be out of range.” Twindo non-residential page says “5m (15ft)” and flags tall ceilings and tall equipment as the cases where range matters. Scaniverse: use a splat if you need “beyond the 5-meter range of the LiDAR scanner.” — [Polycam Space Mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices); [Twindo exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/); [Twindo non-residential](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-want-to-scan-non-residential-environments-will-canvas-work-outside-of-the-home/); [Scaniverse](https://dev.scaniverse.com/support)
- **Sunlight is a tracking problem, with slightly different severity by vendor.** Polycam troubleshooting: “Tracking lost: Too much sunlight or moving too fast.” “Avoid scanning during golden hour or with strong directional sunlight.” Best case they describe is even indoor light. SiteScape: “Avoid scanning outdoors or in direct sunlight.” If you must, “wait for an overcast day” and scan morning or late afternoon when the sun is lower; also watch heat above 75°F (24°C), because a hot phone throttles and “drastically reduc[es] scan performance.” Twindo, updated 14 September 2026: “Bright sunlight is not as much of a factor as it used to be” on current LiDAR iPhones versus older sensors, “However, it is still necessary to avoid shooting directly into the sun.” — [Polycam Space Mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices); [SiteScape best practices](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices); [Twindo exteriors](https://www.twindo.com/support/can-i-use-canvas-to-scan-exteriors/)
- **Blank wall and sky lose tracking.** SiteScape: looking only at “homogeneous objects, like blank walls or the sky, for more than a couple of seconds” can drift and mis-align points. Keep “feature-rich” detail in frame (their texture-spectrum example: brick wall vs plain white wall). Twindo: getting too close so the camera sees only blank wall for many frames loses position; start on a corner with detail (shelves, frames, patterns). RoomPlan `lowTexture`: solid-color wall, or a view with no edges. Apple depth confidence is lower on highly reflective and highly absorptive surfaces. — [SiteScape](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178759-scanning-best-practices); [Twindo non-residential](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-want-to-scan-non-residential-environments-will-canvas-work-outside-of-the-home/); [Twindo room tutorial](https://www.twindo.com/support/tutorial-how-to-scan-a-room-with-canvas/); [lowTexture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession/instruction/lowtexture); [confidenceMap](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/ardepthdata/confidencemap)
- **Movement that fits a person standing at a wall.** Twindo fence-painting: up and down, step sideways, one direction, perimeter of what you can reach, Done. SiteScape: smooth spray, 1–4 m standoff, small rotations. Scaniverse outdoor/street advice: do **not** “pivot around a point like you’re taking a panoramic photo”; move your body; for a large scene, pretend there is an object in the middle so you get different angles; 1–3 minutes. - **Object Capture assumes you can move around a bounded object, including flipping it.** Feedback includes too close, too far, out of field of view, and “not flippable.” The point-cloud review has a turntable gesture and shot locations. None of that requires a walkable loop around a house wall, and the docs do not describe a ground-standing outdoor wall mode. — [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum); [ObjectCapturePointCloudView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturepointcloudview)

### Inferences
- For a meter and the wall beside it, the user can step back and slide along the wall. They often cannot orbit the meter. Scaniverse’s “don’t spin in place” and Twindo’s sideways fence-painting match that constraint. Object Capture’s orbit and magicplan’s “stand still and turn the room green” do not.
- Daylight guidance that transfers: do not aim into the sun; expect weaker depth on shiny metal and on blank siding if the phone is too close; stay inside a few meters; keep a feature (the meter, a corner, a window edge) in view so tracking does not stare at sky or a blank wall.
- Vendors disagree on how bad sun is (SiteScape: avoid outdoors; Twindo 2026: less sensitive, still don’t shoot into the sun). A prototype should treat direct sun as a tracking risk, not as a ban and not as a solved problem.
- “Accurate” in store copy (inch-level, millimeter) is not evidence that a finished outdoor scan met a tolerance.

### Gaps
- No vendor page fetched gives a measured outdoor error for iPhone LiDAR at 1 m vs 5 m in full sun. Twindo’s 1–2% figure is for processed residential CAD versus tape, not an outdoor meter-wall test.
- Twindo’s two range write-ups (16 ft 5 in vs 15 ft) are inconsistent; both point at a 5 m sensor limit.
- Apple’s confidence levels are not mapped to a user-facing distance or lux threshold in the docs.

## What should a prototype copy versus avoid?

### Takeaway
Copy a short, local “still missing” mark and one coaching line at a time (closer, farther, slower, more light, need an edge). Let the user finish a 1–2 minute pass. Do not copy orbit-the-object, a room that must close, a second scrub of the same patch, or any “complete” that is not the same thing as “this wall and the ground in front of it were actually seen.”

### Cited Findings
- Patterns that match a wall the user can walk along, from the sources above: Twindo fence-painting and an explicit **Done** after one direction of travel; Polycam blue-grid-until-covered, haptics while data is coming in, and “rotate your body, not the phone”; SiteScape 1–4 m standoff and “don’t stare at blank wall or sky”; Scaniverse blur-until-sharp and a 1–3 minute cap; RoomPlan’s short instruction set (`moveCloseToWall`, `moveAwayFromWall`, `slowDown`, `turnOnLight`, `lowTexture`); magicplan’s green check only after a wall is recognized, plus **Discard & Rescan** when it is not. — citations in the sections above
- Patterns that fight this task: Object Capture reticle, bounding box, flip, turntable review, and too-close/too-far relative to a free-standing object; magicplan manual mode’s “stay in the same position”; magicplan Auto-Scan trying to **close** an incomplete room so the plan still generates; Polycam/Twindo warnings that **repeat passes** add artifacts; SiteScape’s building-scale progress bar and 10-segment merge (a drift tool for large interiors); any Done state that Polycam’s own blog says still “guesses” missing geometry. — [Object Capture feedback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/objectcapturesession/feedback-swift.enum); [magicplan Manual-Scan](https://help.magicplan.app/scan-a-room-in-seconds-using-lidar); [magicplan Auto-Scan](https://help.magicplan.app/auto-scan-your-floor-plan); [Polycam Space Mode](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/36655587097620-How-to-Use-Space-Mode-with-LiDAR-enabled-devices); [Twindo rejection](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/); [SiteScape multi-scan](https://help.holobuilder.com/en/articles/8178760-using-multi-scan)
- Separating “finished the motion” from “measurements are good” is already how Twindo works: the scan can be saved and measured, while Scan-to-CAD may still reject it. Their 1–2% line is a post-process claim about CAD, and they tell users to tape the few dimensions that must be tighter. — [Twindo accuracy](https://www.twindo.com/support/what-kind-of-accuracy-can-i-expect-from-canvas/); [Twindo rejection](https://www.twindo.com/support/i-got-an-email-saying-my-scans-couldn-t-be-converted-what-gives/)

### Inferences
- For a battery-placement preview, “enough” should mean the meter wall and the ground patch in front of it have been seen from more than one standing position inside a few meters, not that a room mesh closed or an object orbit finished.
- One persistent cue (unscanned tint on that patch, or blur) plus a single line (“step back,” “move along the wall,” “slow down,” “turn so the sun is behind you,” “point at the meter or a corner, not the sky”) matches RoomPlan and Polycam better than a checklist of nags.
- A checkmark or green state should mean that patch was observed, the way magicplan turns a wall green only when recognized. Green should not mean the placement rules passed, and it should not appear because the app auto-filled a hole.
- Do not ask the user to walk around the meter if the wall, fence, or shrubs block the far side. Scaniverse’s statue spiral and Object Capture’s flip are for objects with a path around them.
- Do not use a percentage “complete” unless it is the fraction of the small target patch that has depth, and do not disable leaving the screen. These products leave Done available; they show what is missing. A false complete is worse than an early finish, because Polycam documents that the model will still look plausible by guessing.
- Avoid a second-pass nag on areas already marked seen. Both Polycam and Twindo say that hurts the scan.
- Avoid copying indoor room failure (orange = room will be thrown away) onto an outdoor wall that is not supposed to close. Twindo already prices a single exterior wall without a closed plan.
- Haptics that mean “frames are being kept,” as Polycam describes, are safe. Haptics or a percent that mean “accurate to the inch” are not, because that inch claim is store marketing and Twindo’s own CAD tolerance is a relative 1–2% after human processing, with an explicit refusal of a universal millimeter bound.

### Gaps
- No source fetched is a 1–2 minute outdoor electrical-meter scan. The copy/avoid list is an inference from room, object, and exterior-wall guidance, not a published pattern for this exact task.
- Matterport and any Occupier-specific Canvas UI could not be checked, so they are not part of the copy/avoid list.
